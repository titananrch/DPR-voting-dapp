# Constitutional Principles of the Governance System

## 1. Authority

Authority in this system is derived exclusively from Seat ownership.

- Each Seat represents a constitutional mandate.
- Each Seat grants exactly one vote.
- Seats are represented on-chain as non-transferable NFTs (SeatNFT).
- No address, role, or admin has authority without a Seat.

## 2. Legitimacy

Seat legitimacy originates outside governance through an election or recall process.

- Election and recall authorities are external to governance.
- Governance may update which authority is recognized, but cannot mint seats itself.
- This separation prevents circular legitimacy.

## 3. Equality

All Seats are equal.

- No weighted voting.
- No privileged roles.
- No veto powers.

One Seat equals one vote under all circumstances.

## 4. Transparency

Governance is fully transparent.

- Votes are publicly attributable to Seat IDs.
- Voting results and individual decisions are permanently auditable on-chain.

## 5. Self-Governance

This system governs its own rules.

- Governance parameters (quorum, duration, thresholds) are mutable only through successful proposals.
- No administrator can override governance decisions.

## 6. Proposal Legitimacy

Proposals require political support before voting.

- A proposal must be supported by at least three Seats.
- Supporting Seats must represent multiple parties.
- This ensures coalition-building and prevents spam.

## 7. Execution Finality

Successful proposals are executed automatically and deterministically.

- Rule changes take effect on-chain.
- Execution cannot be vetoed or reversed.

## 8. Emergency Safeguards

An emergency pause exists to protect the system.

- Only governance may activate or deactivate it.
- Pausing affects execution, not participation or voting.

## 9. Immutability

This Constitution is enforced by code.

- Any rule not explicitly mutable by governance is immutable.
- No hidden privileges exist.
